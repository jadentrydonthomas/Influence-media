The decomposition

Where the change in returned money came from

Returned value is three things multiplied together: how many quotes we issued, how big each one was, and what share of that value came back. The ribbon starts at last period’s returned value, steps once for each of the three, and lands on this period’s. The three steps account for the whole move with nothing left over.

Everything else

Every measure, ranked by how far it moved

Bars run right when the measure improved and left when it worsened. Direction is judged, not signed: more quotes, more value and higher conversion read as improvement; longer turnaround and more engineering hours per quote read the other way. Money and counts move in percent and rates move in points, so the two are ranked and drawn on separate scales: every bar means the same thing as the figure printed beside it.

The race

Ahead or behind, at the same point

Quoted value and returned value accumulating week by week in both periods. The gap between the pairs at any week is how far ahead or behind this period is at that point — not at the end.

Equal age

How quickly the work comes back

The honest way to compare a young book against a finished one. At each checkpoint only the quotes that have been open at least that long are asked whether they booked, on both sides. A book quoted three weeks ago is never scored as though it had a year to answer.

The clock

Our side of it, and theirs

How long we take to put a price out, and how long the customer then takes to answer — the two halves of the lifecycle, moved apart so a change in one is not read as a change in the other.

Concentration

How much of the book rests on how few names

Accounts sorted largest first and accumulated. The straight line is a book spread evenly across every account. The further a curve bows above it, the more of the quoted value sits with a handful of customers — which is a risk no total will ever show you.
