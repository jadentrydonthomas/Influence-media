The honest conclusion

Who is growing the most? It depends on the axis, and the data is unambiguous on each one:

  • Most new users, absolute: Robinhood — +1.8M funded customers in 2025 alone, more than Webull's entire international base. Nobody else in the app-broker cohort adds users at that absolute rate.
  • Fastest user growth, %: Interactive Brokers (+34% YoY, and accelerating at 5.2M accounts), moomoo/Futu (+33%, with triple-digit AUM growth in Japan, Canada, Australia, Malaysia) and Trade Republic (Europe's Robinhood: 2M → 10M+ customers in three years, ~+70%/yr). Not Webull (+8%) and not Robinhood (+6–7%).
  • Fastest asset growth: Webull — customer assets +90% YoY (Q1 2026), net deposits +91%. Webull's existing users are getting dramatically more invested even though its user count grows slowly. That is a real, underappreciated signal — engagement-led growth usually precedes account-led growth.
  • Europe specifically: the market your instinct points at is real — retail participation in Europe is structurally rising, and Trade Republic (10M+ customers, €150B assets, 18 markets), Trading 212 (5M+ clients, UK's most-downloaded trading app) and eToro (3.9M funded) prove the demand. But that's exactly the problem for Webull: it arrived in September 2025, in one country (Netherlands), five-plus years behind entrenched, zero-commission, profitable local leaders. Webull's edge — a US-grade options/active-trader platform — is genuinely differentiated in Europe; its brand and licensing footprint are not, yet. The EU's payment-for-order-flow ban (fully in force 2026) also reshapes everyone's economics there, including the incumbents'.
On your "$7 Robinhood" thesis — the poetic part is true: Webull stock closed at ~$7.40 this week, almost exactly Robinhood's 2022 bottom. But the analogy needs surgery. When HOOD was $7, it already had 22.9M funded US customers, ~$1.4B of revenue, billions of net cash and a US scale moat — the market was mispricing assets that already existed. BULL at $7.40 (~$3.4B cap) has 5.1M funded accounts and $571M of revenue: ~30× smaller cap, ~8× smaller revenue than Robinhood today. The market isn't mispricing existing scale; it's declining to pay in advance for international execution that hasn't happened yet — plus a SPAC share structure and recurring political scrutiny of Webull's Chinese origins that HOOD never carried. The bull case is real but different: BULL trades near ~6× sales vs HOOD's ~22×, is already adjusted-operating-profitable (19% margin, revenue +46%), grows assets faster than anyone in the peer set, and is the only app broker with 14 live markets. If international funded accounts (760K, ~15% of total, growing far faster than the US book) compound for 3–4 more years and Europe adds even 2–3 countries' worth of Trade-Republic-style adoption, today's price looks silly. If not, +8% account growth does not "grow past Robinhood" — at current rates the gap in funded accounts widens by ~1.2M per year.

The bigger picture you spotted is correct: trading is becoming culture globally — but the purest proof isn't Webull or Robinhood. It's India (Groww ~13M active investors, Zerodha ~8M, 190M+ demat accounts — a market neither company serves), Japan's NISA boom, Korea's retail wave, and Europe's savings-plan generation. The brokers monetizing that culture-shift fastest today are Trade Republic, IBKR, and Futu/moomoo. Robinhood is the profit machine of the trend; Webull is the leveraged call option on it going global.

What to watch to know if the Webull thesis is working (all reported quarterly): international funded accounts (currently ~760K — needs to sustain >40%/yr), EU market count beyond the Netherlands, net deposits (+91% — the leading indicator), take-rate on the $24B asset base, and whether Korea launches via Meritz in 2026–27. If two consecutive quarters show international accounts stalling, the growth story is Robinhood's and IBKR's, full stop.

Sources & method

Method. "Users" are compared as funded/active accounts (each broker's own definition, noted throughout). Registered-user counts shown only where flagged. Figures marked ≈ are estimates triangulated from company statements, filings and press coverage; year-end values unless noted. No broker discloses city-level users — country-level is the finest resolution that isn't made up; app-download rankings are the usual proxy for sub-national momentum and are directionally consistent with the map above. Map mode 2 ("fastest-growing challenger") is a judgment call from the growth data cited below, limited to the seven tracked challengers — local incumbents (e.g. XP in Brazil, Groww in India, Wealthsimple in Canada) are deliberately shown in the country cards instead. Nothing here is investment advice.
